How this rent vs buy calculator works

This free calculator compares four ways to handle the same money in India: buying a house with a home loan, buying it outright with cash, renting and investing the full amount, or renting and investing the difference between your EMI and rent every month. For each one it adds up everything you pay, from the down payment, stamp duty, registration and brokerage to EMIs, maintenance and rent, and compares that with what you own at the end, after tax.

It also shows the full cost of buying a house with and without a home loan, how much interest you save by paying extra on your loan, and how income tax under the old and new regimes changes the result. Every number is editable, so you can test your own house price, interest rate, rent and expected investment return.

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to buy a house or rent in India?

It depends on three numbers more than anything else: how fast house prices in your area rise, the rent compared with the house price, and the return you can earn by investing the money instead. When yearly rent is a small share of the price and your investments can earn more than the house grows, renting and investing usually comes out ahead. When prices rise steadily and you plan to stay for many years, buying usually wins. The calculator above works this out with your own numbers.

How much does it really cost to buy a house in India?

On top of the price you pay stamp duty and registration (set by each state), GST on under-construction homes, brokerage with GST, legal fees and society charges. A home loan adds a processing fee, legal and valuation fees, mortgage stamp duty in some states, and interest, which is usually the biggest cost of all. The cost table above lists every charge with and without a loan.

How much interest can I save by prepaying my home loan?

Paying even a small amount above your EMI goes straight to the principal, so it can cut lakhs of rupees of interest and close the loan years early. Under RBI rules, banks can't charge a penalty for prepaying a floating-rate home loan taken by an individual. Use the extra payment section above to see your exact savings.

What tax benefits do I get on a home loan?

Under the old tax regime you can deduct home loan interest up to ₹2 lakh a year for a house you live in, and principal repayment under the ₹1.5 lakh Section 80C limit. The new tax regime gives no deduction for a self-occupied house. The calculator includes both, so you can see how much tax you would actually save.

Should I invest in FD or mutual funds instead of buying a house?

That depends on the return you expect and how it's taxed. FD interest is taxed every year at your slab rate, while gains on shares and equity mutual funds are taxed only when you sell, at 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh. Set your expected return and investment type in the calculator to compare them fairly with buying.

Does the type of property affect its value?

Yes. With an independent house you own the full plot, which usually holds value best as the building ages. A flat in a high-rise comes with only a small share of land, so its value depends more on the building. Builder floors sit in between. Building age also matters for loans, repairs and resale.
